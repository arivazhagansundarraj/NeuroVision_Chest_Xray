About Pneumonia

Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs in one or both lungs. The sacs may fill with fluid or pus, causing cough, fever and difficulty breathing.

Medical terms glossary

Pneumonia

Infection that inflames the air sacs (alveoli) in one or both lungs. The sacs may fill with fluid or pus.

Radiograph

The medical term for an X-ray image. A chest radiograph shows the lungs, heart, ribs and airways.

Alveoli

Tiny air sacs in the lungs where oxygen enters the blood and carbon dioxide leaves it.

Opacity

A whiter-than-normal area on a lung X-ray. It can mean fluid, infection or other tissue is blocking the X-rays.

Consolidation

A region of lung tissue filled with liquid instead of air. It is a classic X-ray sign of bacterial pneumonia.

Infiltrate

A general term for substances such as fluid or cells that appear in the lung tissue and look hazy on an X-ray.

Pleural effusion

Fluid that collects in the space between the lung and the chest wall. It often appears as a white area at the base of the lung.

Bronchi

The main airways that carry air from the windpipe (trachea) into each lung.

Dyspnea

The medical word for shortness of breath or difficulty breathing.

Bacterial vs. viral

Bacterial pneumonia often shows lobar consolidation, while viral pneumonia tends to look patchy or diffuse on X-ray.

Sensitivity & specificity

Sensitivity is how often a test finds true disease. Specificity is how often it correctly clears healthy patients.
